> Lo grande de esta sesión: **definición formal de BA** y la anatomía de 3 niveles dentro del core.

## 1. BA definido formalmente (+ familia de retests) [VID-M2020: Session 12 @ 00:01:41–00:03:04]

- "**The area, the inventory, the liquidity base that pushed through the shelf is the breakout accumulation. That's the definition of it.**" (La que empuja el precio A TRAVÉS del shelf hacia midflow: "the BA pushes price through into midflow"; si un intento sale pero no entra en midflow y otro posterior sí, la BA es la del que lo logró. [@ 00:54:27–00:54:49])
- "If price returns back down and retests the BA, we call that the **shelf retest**. It won't always have a BA level there, but it's still classed as the shelf retest."
- Segundo retest = "**the retest of the shelf retest, or the child shelf retest — it's the child of the first one**."
- Variante: si el precio vuelve hasta el low (sin romperlo) y desde ahí continúa, "**now this turns into the BA**".
- Las BAs pueden estar washed/drenadas: "both are BA but the context is different — this one has been drained, this one has not". [@ 00:54:58–00:55:15]

## 2. Anatomía del core: tres niveles dentro [@ 00:03:04–00:04:14]

- "All of these levels fall inside of the **core area of liquidity** of any single price run, which is the **origin**... There'll be **the pickup** (first pickup), some form of **fractal apex retest**, and there's also going to be some **BA** within every single apex that exists."
- "**Every single apex is classed as core liquidity relative to the timeframe of the price run.**"
- Por qué refinar el core en 3 pockets: **riesgo apretado** — mismo dinero arriesgado en 2-3 pips vs 10 pips multiplica el reward. "If you ever see my levels like this: I caught this with a 3-pip stop. **The stop goes directly past the level. Have it pre-planned.**" [@ 00:04:19–00:05:35]

## 3. Reglas nuevas de validación y confirmación

- **Validación del pickup/OP**: "the original pickup is only validated **once price has grabbed the level and it's gone to the shelf**. If price has just gone into the level and it hasn't done anything, it's not classed as a pickup." [@ 00:30:31–00:30:43]
- **La debilidad del shelf se confirma en la RESPUESTA, no en la ruptura**: "the weakness of the shelf isn't necessarily confirmed once price has pushed through — it's actually confirmed in **how price responds off it**." Preferencia: "I would prefer if my first run went through the shelf, compared to held inside the shelf — subtle differences but this increases the probability." [@ 00:12:22–00:13:04]
- **Poke past the low**: "we're going to talk about next session how the probabilities significantly change if price pokes past that low" — y muestra ejemplo donde una invalidación sutil del pickup "shifts the probabilities significantly". [@ 00:30:17–00:32:26]
- Anatomía ≠ características: variaciones visualmente muy distintas comparten las mismas características — "**in every successful apex, the original pickup will hold. The low held. That's it.**" [@ 00:09:53–00:10:08]

## 4. Estilo de entrada: limit vs activa [@ 00:00:41–00:01:09]

- Perdió un trade por dormir sin limit puesta: "the reason I don't like putting limits in: the likelihood of the initial response is higher (than follow-through) and I can manage that response — it puts me in a position where I can view incoming price action to determine if it's likely to continue or fail... **depending on the level as well — this isn't the best of levels**."
- [INFERENCIA] Regla combinada con S13 (limit en fractal apex de calidad): **limit resting solo en niveles top; en niveles menores, entrada activa vigilando la llegada**.

## 5. Stages vs estados (dos taxonomías) [@ 00:13:45–00:18:48]

- Cada swing tiene 3 **stages**: "the apex, the midflow, and the sideways pop — and that reverses on the way down". [@ 00:37:49–00:38:06]
- Los **estados/condiciones de liquidez** (low volume state, inefficiency, exhaustion) "exist regardless of the price swing stage... it can happen during midflow, during apex" — describir ambos por separado.
- Midflow fuerte vs débil (visual): fuerte = "wherever price goes there's always liquidity with it, close to it" (orden grande llenándose); débil = el precio tiene que "reach all the way down past the last inventory to find buyers", drena en el camino. "Whenever you get the biggest moves with the most volume behind them, they always look like this (liquidez pegada al precio)." [@ 00:19:40–00:21:53]

## 6. Edge y niveles malos [@ 00:22:20–00:27:12]

- Nivel residual + washed = "weak times two". "You will never have a consistent edge taking a trade off a level like that. It might be 50/50, might be 60/40... **you'd much rather wait for the 90/10 than the 60/40.**"
- Aunque responda (y respondió: media hora, ~40 pips), "there's no great edge — the outcome is distributing randomly. It could happen the next 10 times, it doesn't mean shit. It's over a very large sample where you get a true reflection of probabilities (**law of large numbers**)."
- "The inexperienced mind will think they missed a trade there. In reality there's no edge in it."
- **Shelf flip aislado no tiene edge**: "just waiting for price to break shelf and then buying it has no edge alone... What we can do is introduce context, introduce variables that increase the likelihood: picking up a core area, the price swing having weakness, having actually broke midflow and done the sideways pop. Then when price breaks that shelf, if you short it THEN, there's a higher probability." [@ 01:24:28–01:25:44]

## 7. Extension fill en midflow contrario + gestión fractal [@ 00:40:00–00:48:07]

- En bullish midflow: "extensions will get filled, but the sell liquidity is not strong enough to power price down. So if I take this extension fill, **the probability of my trade is high only up until the extension fill** — not through the last inventory."
- Timing: esperar a que el fractal se ponga "super super inefficient" hacia el nivel; niveles + price delivery = **solo timing y probabilidad de respuesta**; el tamaño/duración esperable del trade viene del CONTEXTO (HTF + stage). [@ 00:42:53–00:43:55]
- Gestión con CPS fractal tras entrar: "what I need to see is the inventory of buyers fails to push to new highs, then a bearish fractal shelf flip and bearish midflow — the response throughout its duration should reflect weak bids." [@ 00:47:26–00:48:07]

## 8. Microestructura: last look, dealers y oligopolio [@ 00:58:42–01:11:45]

- **Last look** en el agotamiento: "what dealers do at the points of exhaustion is start using last look. If someone tries to buy at the dealer's ask price, they'll **reject it and re-quote at a higher price**. So there's absolutely no sell liquidity at the point of exhaustion." Pasado el shelf, la liquidez se retira algorítmicamente para evitar adverse selection.
- Los dealers ajustan quotes por gestión de inventario y por miedo a traders informados (hedge fund ejemplo): "the change in the ask price is not because the underlying value changed but because of the dealer's behavior — **that's a market inefficiency**" → el precio se estira más de lo que debería → pockets of air → "what it takes to push price down through that gap is little volume, over and over".
- Estructura del mercado FX: ~15 investment banks + non-bank market makers formando competencia imperfecta (analogía del oligopolio de la leche); poder asimétrico (rechazar trades, slippage, spreads).
- Conclusión operativa: "**we're trading that volatility. That volatility is predictable.** I don't give two shits if cable's at 150 or 120 in a month — what I do know is on the way there it will behave in a very consistent way."
- Contra la predicción: participantes con más información siempre; unknown unknowns; retail usando charts históricos = "looking at a coin flip chart and seeing trends on it". [@ 01:12:00–01:16:16]

## 9. Proceso / tareas [@ 00:55:26–00:56:33, 01:26:53–01:35:26]
