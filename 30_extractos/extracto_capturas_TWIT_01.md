## Evidencia externa citada por EM (académica/institucional)

- [IMG-TWIT: Screenshot_2] (14-feb): EM cita paper del **Bank of Canada**: "informational asymmetries based on geography arise from the market making capacity of dealers and the customer order flow that dealers capture" — glosa de EM: "**LPs know more about price than orders coming in + have capabilities to exploit that**... banks don't need to 'engineer' your stop on your broker's internal book through black magic — **they can just scalp flow**."
- [IMG-TWIT: Screenshot_3] (19-feb-2020): EM cita **estudio Stanford cs229 (2011) con datos EBS** ("main exchange of EURUSD"): "how **price goes to the weaker side of bid/ask depth — whichever side had the imbalance**". Glosa: "you can have **same volume hitting either side of the spread but price still goes lower or higher, depending on where there's less passive liquidity**. Then the question is what does price look like when there's weakness on one side of the spread?" Ejemplo: reacción de cable al dato de inflación. "Ur shitty ECN depth means nothing" (EBS = fuente mayorista). → **Soporte empírico directo del principio 2**.
- [IMG-TWIT: Screenshot_8]: abstract de **Roel Oomen (2016), "Execution in an aggregator"** (co-head eFX spot de Deutsche Bank): agregadores, last look ("reject rate due to the so-called 'last-look' trade acceptance process"), winner's curse/adverse selection de los LPs, y cómo el estilo de ejecución del trader determina el impacto. → Fuente académica del mecanismo **last look** que EM enseña en M2020 S12.
- [IMG-TWIT: Screenshot_13]: página de paper (Farmer et al., sobre clustered volatility y heavy tails): "the dominant drivers of both heavy tails and clustered volatility are due to changes in the frequency with which transactions generate non-zero price returns... These variables fluctuate because of **changes in the balance between liquidity taking and liquidity provision**... the balance between liquidity taking and liquidity provisions is key... **This is typically more important than the number of transactions or their size.**" → Soporte empírico del principio 1 (volatilidad = f(liquidez), no del volumen).
