## 1. Midflow: definición por inventarios y primera ruptura

- **Definición:** "We classify midflow as a **directional price move of imbalance**, with the characteristic of the structure that **the inventory that's pushing price holds. The first break of inventory shows a sign of the weakness of that momentum.**" [VID-M2020: Session 13 @ 00:05:44–00:05:59]
- Statement a validar (de EM): "**when the first inventory breaks, it typically won't continue for a long while after that**." [@ 00:06:00–00:06:12] "After the breaks of midflow there's a lack of sustainability in that price run, it doesn't often continue." [@ 00:02:01–00:02:06]
- Run fuerte = aguanta TODAS las bases: "it holds every single base... the first time we break past the last inventory: indicative of strength and sell momentum slowing down." [@ 00:01:36–00:02:36]
- Patrón mayoritario: "most of the successful price swings follow this pattern: liquidity holding price down throughout the duration of its strength, then as the momentum dries off, price will break midflow and it will pop, leaving this area weak." PERO "doesn't translate into every single instance of break of midflow causing a reversal — **it's just highly probabilistic**." [@ 00:02:54–00:03:36]
- Señal sutil de fuerza/debilidad del midflow: en midflow fuerte el precio "hardly pokes back INTO the shelf"; reaching/poking past the shelf = debilidad y sell liquidity fina. [@ 00:06:30–00:06:53]
- Variaciones: el precio puede volver al shelf o no volver (la sell liquidity persigue precio agresivamente); en ambas variantes **el último shelf aguanta**. No se clasifica midflow por "volver al origen". [@ 00:05:00–00:05:44]
- El midflow es el building block de la ineficiencia: dentro de un movimiento muy ineficiente hay midflow characteristics a escala menor ("stability, surge, drawback in, stability"). [@ 01:10:12–01:10:24]

## 2. Jerarquía de timeframes (principios nuevos, dictados para anotar)

- "**Higher time frame pulls more liquidity**" y "**higher time frame governs lower time frame / takes precedence**" — "those principles you want to write down, they're related." [@ 00:13:52–00:14:17]
- "A lower time frame shelf has no influence on the higher time frame. If it's a H1 price run, **you need a H1 shelf flip**. You will see many LTF apex formations and shelf flips that fail to materialize time and time again." [@ 00:13:13–00:13:38]
- "Until the bulk of the liquidity of the H1 run dries off, any fractal price run breaks are somewhat inconsequential — they're not going to combat a H1 midflow. **What they do do: that fractal shelf flip is what I would use, if I was short, to fill this extension** (counter flow)." [@ 00:14:19–00:15:07]
- Para fadear una pierna fractal: es un price swing completo a su escala — "I could expect the complete features to turn up... as long as each of the components remain favourable" (apex favorable, shelf flip no "shitty", break de midflow + pop, sideways pop en el apex fractal). "But what I won't do is use that 5-minute shelf as validation to hold for a H1 reversal." [@ 00:15:28–00:16:30]
- "**View your price swings relative to the timeframe they're present on. A one minute swing has one minute features, the daily swing has daily features.**" [@ 00:17:41–00:17:50] Aparente contradicción resuelta: se analiza el swing en su TF (para no mezclar escalas) pero se selecciona/temporiza siendo consciente de dónde encaja en el contexto mayor ("this price swing is in the parabolic phase of this larger price swing"). [@ 00:20:11–00:21:18]
- Nivel HTF = más tiempo construyéndose = más liquidez = mayor probabilidad de respuesta grande; nivel de 1min = poca. [@ 00:16:40–00:17:06]
- Timing fino con fractales incluso en runs diarios: no esperar el daily shelf flip para ir corto; anticipar el final del swing con las fases (sideways → pop) en el fractal. [@ 00:18:00–00:19:57]

## 3. Secuencia de fases y anticipación

- Ciclo completo enumerado: shelf flip → shelf retest → midflow (bases aguantan) → primer break de midflow → **sideways** (drena inventario: "multiple pick-offs draining the inventory") → **pop → parabolic phase** → apex formation (incluye la parabólica) → shelf flip → shift → nuevo midflow. [@ 00:18:38–00:19:19, 00:22:55–00:23:27]
- "**After the sideways comes the pop.**" [@ 00:19:30–00:19:35] Tras el sideways se puede anticipar que la parabólica lidera "the final run before price draws back into the shelf and hopefully reverses" → enfocar el timing ahí. [@ 00:19:44–00:19:57]
- "**We typically don't see the parabolic leg be a leg of significance** — it doesn't typically push to new lows (beyond); we actually see a reversal and a shift after the parabolic leg." [@ 01:23:47–01:24:00]
- El primer break de shelf/midflow típicamente golpea un nivel residual, no un área fuerte — eso ayuda a decidir si esperar reversal o continuación. [@ 01:29:40–01:29:49]
- El tipo de nivel que golpeó el high antes de romper shelf influye en si el high aguanta: residual → no esperar que aguante; core/buen nivel → mayor probabilidad. [@ 01:28:30–01:28:57]
- Break de midflow SIN sideways pop previo → probabilísticamente no es el reversal todavía. [@ 01:29:00–01:29:40]
- "When price does break midflow, before it continues to new highs, **it looks like an apex anyway**" → prohibido tener expectativas rígidas; ampliar el reference point con variaciones (Bayesian). [@ 01:26:45–01:28:02]

## 4. Apexes

- Características del apex favorable: "the low (high) holds, the (apex) retest holds, the shelf fails to push to new lows, we push through the shelf and hopefully **stabilise above it** before we shift." [@ 00:58:02–00:58:36]
- Apex más fuerte: "price should have pushed through shelf in the first go; the apex retest typically shouldn't even reach that far back into the pickup if the (opposing) liquidity is super strong." [@ 00:58:06–00:58:20]
- **Consolidation apex**: combinación de debilidad en el movimiento a favor + fuerza core del lado contrario; el shelf ya no tiene "punching power". [@ 01:01:08–01:02:02, 01:05:51–01:06:13]
- Apex que absorbe toda la liquidez al salir: al retornar al nivel "any response off there will usually be weak and price will end up moving through it anyway. **The more we test the level, the weaker it gets — from a finite quantitative point of view the liquidity is being drained.**" [@ 01:04:28–01:05:24]
- Anuncio: **template con las 4 variaciones principales de apex** + gestión de trades en apex ("when am I going to cut, the different options") para "next session". [@ 01:45:10–01:45:43] → localizar en qué sesión cayó realmente (¿15?).

## 5. Los 3 lados de la liquidity base (resuelve pregunta abierta n.º 3)

- **Liquidity base** definida: "simply an area where price was stable and trade was being facilitated, and then you typically have a surge out from it. It can grind out — but in either case the liquidity state shifts." [@ 01:02:13–01:02:43]
- "All types of liquidity bases, whether a fractal core or a residual base, have some form of liquidity in them and **there's three sides to it: a top, a middle and a bottom.**" [@ 01:03:05–01:03:27]
- Mecánica: "If price reaches to the **bottom** of the base, all the liquidity there can be expected to have gone. The only remaining liquidity would be in the **last area that grabbed it** (the pickup) — and we wouldn't expect it to be strong." [@ 01:03:29–01:03:48]
- **OP definido explícitamente: "we actually call the pickup of a base the OP, the original pickup."** [@ 01:04:15–01:04:25]
- La forma de SALIR del nivel importa: si absorbió toda la liquidez al salir, al retorno no habrá combustible para otro movimiento. [@ 01:04:28–01:04:44]

## 6. Entornos de precio (más allá de trend/range)

- "Simply calling something a trend or a range isn't appropriate... we have one trend and two trends but they're entirely different: one is highly inefficient and one is relatively stable/modestly efficient." [@ 00:23:56–00:24:15]
- "**Price environments are changing relentlessly**... Those changes, however subtle, directly affect: the levels you can use, the duration of the trade, how big the response is likely to be, the frequency of trades, and how you have to manage." [@ 00:25:00–00:25:47]
- Características (vocabulario para diferenciar):
  - **Midflow rápido/ineficiente**: "short extension fills or hardly filling the extension back to the inventory, sometimes not even responding off residual liquidity, lows aren't holding, levels hit are residual." [@ 00:53:20–00:53:52]
  - **Midflow lento/eficiente**: "larger extension fills, price draws back further, the fractal swings are more defined." [@ 00:53:54–00:54:15]
  - "If you're a breakout trader you only want the (inefficient) midflow; if you're a counter trend trader you only want the (stable) one — even though both are classified as midflow." [@ 00:54:24–00:54:42]
- Tres métricas a evaluar siempre: **volatilidad, estado de liquidez, fase del price swing** ("volatility is a function of available liquidity" las conecta; bajo ellas: price delivery / HOW price moves). [@ 00:56:47–00:57:33]
- "After any shot of high volume, **volume stabilizes. All the time.** It's actually an exploitable price behavior." [@ 00:46:21–00:46:39]
- Entornos event-driven (COVID, Brexit, 2008, flash crash ene-2019): los movimientos HTF más rápidos son todos event-driven; mecanismo: la liquidez no comparece (LPs/fondos no transaccionan) + hedging masivo de exposición + HFTs "jump the queue in liquidity" + flujo normalmente pasivo se vuelve agresivo → volumen unidireccional compuesto. Cuando el evento se seca, vuelve la estabilidad. [@ 00:38:40–00:43:29]
- Por qué el precio es estable la mayoría del tiempo: demanda de liquidez two-sided no especulativa (hedging, operacional) + cultura de minimización de costes → ejecución por algos que trocean y ocultan ("they don't just fill a $50 million order in one ticket, they break it into small pieces, hide it, fill it over different venues"). [@ 00:43:35–00:45:29] → coincide con [PAPER: HSBC FX LS].
- Riesgo de cola: en flash crash un stop puede ejecutarse mucho más abajo — "you can lose your whole account in a single flash crash, plus negative." [@ 01:51:56–01:52:14]
- "The market is **efficiently inefficient**." No hay edge en timeframes sub-segundo vs HFTs; "you can't trade breakouts anymore — by the time the breakout is seen on our screen the volatility is finished." [@ 02:06:35–02:07:39]
