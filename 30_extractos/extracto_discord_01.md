## [DISCORD: Disc 2] — stops vs liquidez fragmentada; delta; ventaja estructural de los LPs

- GrifoneTrader (defendiendo stop cascades): "stop cascades just materialise into order flow... think about a news event where the book gets super light beforehand, you pop some stops, it's easy to pop more, few will be using limits in that scenario."
- **EssFX corrige el énfasis**: "Grifones got a point btw harry, **price movement in fast markets is largely influenced by fragmenting liquidity rather than stops. In other words you can trigger stops but if some spec has large bids there its likely LP will take on the risk and deal it off the spread.**" → matiza el esquema stop-run de Tilopa: los stops son combustible secundario; lo primario es la fragmentación.
- Sobre delta divergence (a trader_umr): "**delta divergence is just spotting liquidity absorbing flows**, often its speculative buyside players that do it. **Like everything in trading, information is not a trading signal — requires judgement. Info = tool.** so imo its valuable af, though its not needed, **can be seen in price**."
- Sobre mercado-específico: "why are all of you so interested in market specific dynamics? **because it explains the behaviour of each market. Some of you are talking about LPs in equities and others in FX — they're worlds apart.** Whole point should be centred around the **implication of market organisation on prices observed** — that forces you to differentiate between prices in centrally traded assets and in fx."
- harry: "fill the stops at a price below the speculator's bid, then sell that inventory back to the speculator?" → EssFX: "If possible yeah. **Thats advantage LPs in fx have: they're counterparties to all transactions being placed on their book. Its that same structure that lets them see if they want to hold onto risk or not.**"

## [DISCORD: Disc 7, 8] — qué para un run; urgencia; taxonomía por evento

- EssFX: "**this is all you need to stop a price run** [pocket de bids]. What's usually left behind in the move lower is **dispersed liquidity, or 'vacuum'. Depending on depth, you could have more market selling than buying and price still goes up** — unless ofcourse **some entity aggressively moves limit prices lower (hint)**." ¿Cuándo? "if entity is urgent for whatever reason — **doesnt happen all that often**. in what instance would you accept lower and lower prices?... maybe if youre exposed to risk from recent event. **so event driven volatility is important.**"
- Taxonomía con fechas (selling POV): "10-oct cable [headline Brexit]: move of **intent, with urgency**, initially anyway. Sell-off jun-ago: **momentum but less urgency, more passive**. March this year: **one sided liquidity**."
- Diferencia fuerza urgent vs one-sided: "a run usually dries off after it stretches and hits opposing liquidity. **One sided liquidity means even if market orders drying off, theres liquidity there holding price.**" (harry: "first one is price sprinting — vulnerable to gassing out; 2nd is a sustained grind".) EssFX: "**price only sustains a grind if theres urgency — unreasonable to expect some guy to keep hitting the bid forever. market orders will always taper off.**" Urgencia pasiva definida: "cus of how aggressively it chases price — **price moves up a hundred pips, bids populate the book up there. thats urgency.**" ¿Por qué no market order si hay urgencia? "**cus you care about execution cost.**"
