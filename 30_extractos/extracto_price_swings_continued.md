## 1. CPS framework [PDF: Price Swings Continued, p.2]

- "Price movement is a function of available liquidity & **the CPS framework is what we use to navigate & trade price**."
- "The CPS clearly defines components of price which we can categorise throughout the duration of a swing formation to better contextualise what price is doing & connect with individual pieces of price behaviour."
- "This isolation of price features later helps hone in on **which parts of price you have a better expectancy trading** & others less so."
- "**The CPS framework is a representation of HOW price moves rather than why price moves (!!!) — you want to know it inside out.**"
- A internalizar: shifting points del swing, variaciones anatómicas, desarrollo de un estado de volatilidad a otro, características relativas, respuestas a áreas del lado izquierdo, fractal.
- Hay que poder explicar cada fase desde liquidity dynamics — cómo el CPS se relaciona con los liquidity principles.

## 2. Anatomía anotada de un price run A–B [IMG: Price Swings Continued p.3, GBPUSD 1min]

Secuencia completa anotada sobre un run alcista A→B y su reversión:

1. **Core liquidity base of price run A–B** (rectángulo azul en el origen del run).
2. **"Breakout accumulation, shifting point. Top, middle & bottom generated responses (orange circles)"** → **BA = Breakout Accumulation, confirmado**. Es un punto de shift, y sus partes alta/media/baja generan respuestas posteriores.
3. "Residual liquidity from the up move, weak, bottomside unfilled, top picked" — la liquidez residual que deja la subida.
4. "Price response, skinny leg extension into residual, unfilled liquidity & price fills to closest sell liquidity base (green rectangle)".
5. **Shelf** (rectángulo naranja bajo el máximo) y **"Apex Base & Apex Base Retest"** (círculo verde en el máximo).
6. "**Shift in state of liquidity**, offers cause momentum change. Price drawing lower through liquidity void left behind by bids (yellow tint rectangle) & gaps through on the way down too" → etiqueta **Gapslip**.
7. "Weak responses on the way back down".
8. "Largest bullish price response since price swing shifted to downside: price responded off core liquidity base bottom (blue rectangle)" — la base core del run genera la mayor respuesta al retorno.
9. Leyenda: "Price swing: black horizontal = Apex Base; blue double arrow = core liquidity base response". "Sellers pushing with conviction, representative of market intent".

## 4. Midflow [PDF: p.7 + IMG: p.5, p.6]

- "Midflow stage comes after a shift in liquidity (price picks up level, absorbs shelf & momentum shift takes place)." [PDF p.7]
- "Forms in an area of weak opposing liquidity whilst momentum liquidity is stronger (price apexes out at a top, gets past shelf and starts running lower through residual buy liquidity)." [PDF p.7]
- "Categorised as **stair step structure**, weak responses off opposing liquidity (extension, fill, new range, continuation extension, fill, new range etc)." [PDF p.7]
- "Will typically continue until price becomes inefficient OR starts to absorb available inventory momentum liquidity which can signal the run is weakening." [PDF p.7]
- "**Midflow is an EXPECTATION** with very specific characteristics & preceding price swing stages — we can either match developing price with these characteristics as a continuation pattern OR **when price fails to move into midflow stage it is a reason to reassess trade idea**." [PDF p.7]
- "Midflow is the formation of smaller price swings inside the bigger one (fractal) and can be used to take trades with or against the flow depending on trading style." [PDF p.7]
- "In a midflow state, any move off a residual area against the flow/momentum is subject to drying off and the flow to continue pressuring until the momentum liquidity & price run dries off." [PDF p.7]
- Esquema MIDFLOW STRUCTURE [IMG p.5]: "Shelf & shift in state of liquidity → price extends into residual buy bases, responds but move gets absorbed in nearest area of sell orderflow. This is the typical midflow characteristics we look for AFTER shift in state of liquidity post apex & shelf flip." Rectángulos rojos = respuestas absorbidas; "residual, weak, left over liquidity".
- En H4 [IMG p.6]: "Black rectangles represent the **post-shift stage**. This price behaviour typically follows a apex base retest & shift past shelf. Gold rectangles represent shelf." + "MIDFLOW: after price pushes past shelf, the bulk inventory of a particular price move, it causes a shift in the momentum/state of liquidity. Price then runs through residual, left over liquidity which are weak areas thus the responses off of those bases are also weak. The responses get absorbed in the near area of deal flow and the move continues = midflow."

## 5. Shelf liquidity & apexes [PDF: p.8 + IMG: p.9–p.15]

- "**Shelf liquidity = bulk of the momentum pushing any single price move, every price swing has one.** As price is running along, it continuously feeds into the nearest shelf, picks liquidity up and continues the price run until the shelf isn't strong enough to continue supporting the run." [PDF p.8]
- "Price pushing past a shelf after picking up opposing liquidity causes a **shift in the state of liquidity** where the opposite side of the market takes control." [PDF p.8]
- "**Shelf is RELATIVE to the timeframe** ie a 5min shelf has no bearing on a H1+ price swing... the smaller shelves are the inventory of the fractal price swings inside the big one (important for trade management)." [PDF p.8]
- "**An apex & shelf flip typically precedes a shift & midflow.** Once a particular swing has caused a shift the apex area can be expected to generate price responses even if price later moves through it. **An apex that holds inside the shelf without draining or invalidating it is less likely to hold price down** (Needs your own validation, go out and test this rather than rely on face value)." [PDF p.8]
- Semanal/diario anotado [IMG p.9]: shelf = "bulk of buyers/sellers in there holding price up/down"; tras romperlo: "shift, buyers/sellers took control". "Notice how all the shelfs aren't the same, nor are they clean... keep marking price swings and you'll start to see these features in more detail."
- "Not all shelves are the same. The timeframe the price run is present on will have its own shelf for that scale. **Bigger shelf flips = bigger shifts in momentum. But bigger shelfs = hold more liquidity — more work has to be done to get past them thus more time is involved.**" [PDF p.10]
- Big Shelf & Small Shelf [PDF p.11]: distinguir features LTF vs HTF (shelfs, apexes, runs, swings); "can target the smaller swings in large HTF apexes etc (comes after step 3 — broad non-contextual exercising)".
- Mismo apex en distintos TF [PDF p.12]: "On the 1min you see apexes inside the broad apex... there's one predominant apex of the HTF price run. HTF apexes will pool more liquidity. We like to refine pockets of liquidity in HTF pockets, which can be done using the LTF. Drawing & refining levels of unfilled liquidity will be covered later."
- Apexes fractales [IMG p.13]: "Each one of these fractal apexes bear patterns of self similarity to each other regardless of scale or pip distance. **Apexes are areas where a price run meets opposing liquidity, generally in a weak way, and price rebalances lower to shelf liquidity.** Whether the apex retest materially pushes price down and effects a momentum shift is a different story."
- Daily apexes [IMG p.14]: "The key thing is to know **WHERE & WHEN a HTF run is likely to dry off (HTF pocket of liquidity)** with the anatomy of the HTF run coinciding with what a top (or bottom) looks like including **parabolic phase** etc. Also for the HTF run to fill and shift the big shelf has to flip (takes time) creating long and short opportunities (opposing HTF liquidity & HTF shelf liquidity) inside broader apexes which you wouldn't trade on LTFs."
- Esquema Daily [IMG p.15]: "Daily price run / Daily shelf / HTF sell liquidity base. HTF apex with smaller apexes (red circles) & LTF price swings inside the apex. **Can take longs and shorts but with HTF context considered, the play would be to hold shorts as bids inside shelf are weak, the HTF price run is likely dry.**"
